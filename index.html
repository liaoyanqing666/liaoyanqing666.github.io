I am Siyuan Li, a Ph.D. student in Computer Science in UGA LLM Lab at the University of Georgia, advised by Prof. Tianming Liu. Alongside my doctoral work, I am a visiting Ph.D. student at the CAMCA Laboratory at Harvard Medical School and Massachusetts General Hospital, working with Prof. Xiang Li. Before joining UGA, I earned my Bachelor's degree in Artificial Intelligence from Shanghai University, graduating first in my major.

My research focuses on reasoning and post-training in large language models (LLMs) and multimodal large language models (MLLMs), especially understanding where they struggle and how to improve them in practice. My earlier work explored medical instruction tuning, reasoning-trace generation, multimodal retrieval, and cross-modal alignment. Building on that experience, I am now developing benchmarks for clinical and visual reasoning to study models' decision-making, causal reasoning, and planning abilities. In the future, I would also like to devote more effort to model self-evolution and AI agent research, because I believe external guidance is approaching its limits, while models that learn on their own could open up more possibilities.

Outside of my academic work, I am also someone with lots of ideas who enjoys making things. I enjoy turning unusual ideas into working software and research projects, and learning by building things from scratch. Imaging-Centered Artificial Intelligence for Precision Cancer Immunotherapy: Multimodal Biomarkers, Treatment-Effect Estimation, and Clinical Translation

Paper Link · Research Square Preprint (2026). DOI: 10.21203/rs.3.rs-10468208/v1. Under review at Meta-Radiology.

View author list

Shengyang Zhong*, Siyuan Li*, Wanrong Yang, Zheng Zheng, Zhengliang Liu, Jin Xie, Jin Li, Bin Yi, Lin Zhao, Xiang Li, Wei Liu, Lian Zhang, Tianming Liu, Ning Qiang.

Overview: This review examines how imaging can connect changes in tumors with molecular and clinical data to inform cancer immunotherapy decisions. It evaluates the strength and treatment relevance of existing evidence, using radiotherapy plus immunotherapy to highlight a central gap: predicting outcomes under a given treatment does not establish whether a different treatment would benefit the patient.

ASTELD: A Six-Axis Classification Framework for Autonomous AI Agents - Design, Evaluation, and an OpenClaw Case Study

Paper Link · arXiv Preprint (2026). arXiv:2608.05201.

View author list

Siyuan Li, Peng Shu, Churan Yu, Peilong Wang, Ruidong Zhang, Bowen Guo, Xinliang Li, Ruiyu Yan, Arif Hassan Zidan, Yi Pan, Wei Ruan, Lifeng Chen, Junhao Chen, Zhaojun Ding, Yiwei Li, Zhengliang Liu, Haixing Dai, Lin Zhao, Yu Bao, Xiang Li, Wei Zhang, Tianming Liu.

Overview: ASTELD organizes autonomous AI agents by architecture, security, tool integration, execution, human control, and deployment. Its comparison of eight platforms and the OpenClaw ecosystem maps common design patterns and security trade-offs, providing a consistent framework for understanding how agent systems differ beyond individual product features.

The Study of Human Preference Based on Integrated Analysis of N1 and LPP Components
